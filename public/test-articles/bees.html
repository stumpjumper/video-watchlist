How Bees Know Where They Are

A honeybee that finds a patch of flowers can fly back to the hive and, within minutes, communicate its exact location to thousands of other bees using nothing but movement. The waggle dance, as biologists call it, encodes both direction and distance. The bee waggles along a straight line at an angle that corresponds to the direction of the food source relative to the sun, and the duration of the waggle phase tells the others how far away it is. It is one of the most sophisticated non-human communication systems ever discovered.

Bees also maintain a mental map built from landmarks, the position of the sun, and an internal clock that compensates for the sun's movement across the sky throughout the day. If you capture a bee and release it miles away, it can often still find its way home by recalculating from scratch.

What makes this even more remarkable is that the honeybee brain contains fewer than a million neurons. A human brain has roughly eighty-six billion. Navigation, memory, social coordination, and symbolic communication all packed into something the size of a sesame seed.
